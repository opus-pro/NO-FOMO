Microsoft only lets you opt out of AI photo scanning 3x a year

Microsoft's OneDrive service is reportedly initiating trials for a new AI-powered photo scanning feature, specifically incorporating face-recognizing artificial intelligence. This functionality is being rolled out to a limited number of preview users, allowing the system to identify and potentially categorize individuals within uploaded photographs. A notable aspect of this new implementation, and one that has drawn significant attention, is Microsoft's restrictive opt-out policy. Users are permitted to decline participation in this AI photo scanning process only three times per year. This limitation has sparked discussions regarding user data privacy, autonomy, and the extent of control individuals have over their personal digital assets within cloud services. Critics argue that such a policy could effectively coerce users into accepting continuous AI surveillance of their photo libraries after exhausting their limited opt-out allowance, raising ethical questions about consent and the persistent monitoring capabilities of cloud-based AI technologies. The development highlights ongoing tensions between technological advancement, user convenience, and fundamental privacy rights in the digital age.

Qwen3-VL

Qwen3-VL represents the latest and most powerful vision-language model in the Qwen series, offering substantial upgrades across multiple modalities. It delivers superior text understanding and generation, deeper visual perception and reasoning, an extended context length of 256K (expandable to 1M), and enhanced comprehension of spatial and video dynamics. Key capabilities include acting as a visual agent for PC/mobile GUIs, boosting visual coding by generating code from images/videos, and advanced spatial perception for 2D/3D grounding. The model also features long context and video understanding, multimodal reasoning for STEM/Math, upgraded omni-recognition of diverse entities, and expanded OCR supporting 32 languages. Its architectural innovations, like Interleaved-MRoPE and DeepStack, further refine long-horizon video reasoning and image-text alignment.

SurfSense

SurfSense is a highly customizable AI research agent designed to integrate with a user's personal knowledge base, enhancing research capabilities beyond tools like NotebookLM and Perplexity. It connects to a wide array of external sources including search engines, collaboration platforms (Slack, Jira, Notion), and various media sources (YouTube, Luma). Key features include robust support for uploading over 50 file formats, powerful semantic and full-text search, and natural language interaction with saved content to provide cited answers. The platform supports privacy through local LLM integration with Ollama and is self-hostable. Advanced RAG techniques are central, utilizing over 100 LLMs, 6000+ embedding models, multiple rerankers, hierarchical indices, and hybrid search with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, all accessible via a RAG as a Service API backend. Additionally, SurfSense offers a blazingly fast podcast generation agent capable of converting chat conversations into audio content using various TTS providers. A cross-browser extension facilitates saving web pages, even those behind authentication.
