Speculations Concerning the First Ultraintelligent Machine (1965)

This seminal 1965 paper by I. J. Good introduces the theoretical foundation of the technological singularity through the concept of the 'ultraintelligent machine.' Good defines this entity as a machine that can far surpass all the intellectual activities of any human. Since the design of machines is one of these intellectual activities, an ultraintelligent machine could design even better machines, leading to an intelligence explosion. The paper explores early paradigms of artificial intelligence, speculating on the construction of artificial neural networks, the role of subassemblies in brain function, and the necessity of teaching machines rather than writing programs manually. (source: https://languagelog.ldc.upenn.edu/myl/Good1964.pdf)
