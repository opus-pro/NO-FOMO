Context Language Models let models edit their own memory

Context Language Models treat context as a file that a model can directly update, moving memory management into model behavior. The authors report improved accuracy with lower compute on search and long-running agent tasks, including 11.4 percent higher accuracy with 21.5 percent fewer FLOPs on BrowseComp-Plus. They also explore learned context-management strategies and serving optimizations. The results suggest that deciding what to retain can matter as much as enlarging the context window.

LongLive-Plug reuses video distillation across downstream models

LongLive-Plug proposes distilling video-generation capabilities once per backbone family and reusing them as LoRA adapters across compatible downstream models. The capabilities include few-step sampling, single-pass guidance, and error correction for long autoregressive generation. The authors report deployment without target-specific training across 54 models, three backbone families, and eight task categories. This could reduce repeated specialization costs, with applicability depending on compatibility rather than an assumption that every video model can share the same adapters.

03Sep 28

CorpusMap organizes agent search around recurring entities

CorpusMap builds an offline navigation layer that links recurring entities to documents mentioning them. Instead of repeatedly searching a flat collection, an agent can traverse entity pages to gather related evidence across sources. Evaluations with seven models and three datasets report better evidence discovery and answer quality while using fewer tokens on average. The work makes reusable corpus structure a practical alternative to rediscovering document relationships during every individual search task.

04Sep 28

EasyPPO addresses critic instability in LLM reinforcement learning

EasyPPO identifies critic training as a source of instability in PPO for language models. Its changes retain truncated rollouts for critic learning, normalize regression by prompt-level return noise, and use moderately smaller critic minibatches. Across coding, mathematical reasoning, and multi-turn search, the authors report stable training and improvements over several PPO variants. The study emphasizes careful treatment of learning signals and variance rather than replacing the entire reinforcement-learning framework.

EngiWorld exposes gaps in professional engineering agents

EngiWorld evaluates agents on 1,301 expert-curated tasks across six engineering domains and 26 professional software platforms. Its artifact-centered checks assess geometry, physical feasibility, and rule compliance rather than relying only on conversational answers. Among seven frontier models, the strongest reaches an EngiScore of 44.3, while only 3.6 percent of multi-software attempts succeed. The findings show how difficult complete industrial design workflows remain despite progress on general computer-use tasks.

WorldLine predicts robot action outcomes with visual simulation

WorldLine separates transferable manipulation dynamics from embodiment-specific action grounding in a visual simulator. It learns from more than 10,000 hours of action-free robot video and over 2,000 hours of action trajectories, using an image-space interface across embodiments. The authors report 74 percent mean trajectory-success prediction accuracy and planning improvements in evaluated settings. The contribution is a simulator aimed at action fidelity and policy evaluation, with reported gains bounded by the tested environments.

08Sep 25

VoxMem finds persistent gaps in audio memory

VoxMem evaluates memory beyond speech transcripts, including speaker identity, vocal cues, and environmental sounds across sessions. The benchmark contains 3,196 evaluation instances grounded in 34,743 spoken sessions totaling 177 hours. Across 15 large audio-language models, none exceeds 40 percent at a 32K context budget. Models remember words better than acoustic evidence, highlighting a gap for assistants expected to recall who spoke, how something sounded, and how interactions changed over time.
