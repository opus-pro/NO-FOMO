François Chollet challenges an argument for AI consciousness

François Chollet challenges the inference that because consciousness involves computation, computational AI models are therefore likely conscious. He compares that reasoning with treating a rock as alive merely because living organisms also contain atoms. His October 4 post drew more than two thousand likes. The contribution is a critique of an argument, rather than experimental evidence about sentience, and sharpens the distinction between shared ingredients and demonstrated properties in current AI debates.

Gary Marcus questions the leap from AI investment to GDP forecasts

Gary Marcus warns against presenting a plausible increase in AI capital expenditure and a much more speculative doubling of GDP as if both had the same likelihood. His October 4 post frames this as a recurring rhetorical move in technology discussions. The practical distinction is between money committed to infrastructure and economic gains actually realized. The post offers criticism of forecasting rhetoric, not a quantitative model proving what future growth will be.

Nathan Lambert questions whether frontier pacing is workable

Nathan Lambert says pacing frontier AI development sounds attractive in principle but is difficult to implement. He asks which benchmarks would be restricted and who would participate, and points to research on swarms and efficiency as alternative directions if capability work slows.
