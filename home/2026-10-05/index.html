FrugalEvo optimizes program evolution for cost

FrugalEvo splits program evolution between a stronger model that explores strategies and a cheaper model that implements and refines code. The researchers also design prompts and execution structure to improve prefix-cache reuse, evaluating progress against cumulative model cost rather than iteration count alone. They report better budget-aware performance on nine of ten mathematical and systems tasks. The work offers a concrete approach to making automated optimization useful under a fixed spending budget.

02Oct 1

Skill2Real transfers executable robot skills from simulation

Skill2Real learns robot skills through a shared API and a proposer, verifier, and governor loop. Local manipulation skills are learned first, then composed into task-level behavior, with the resulting memories transferred to real robots without task-policy fine-tuning. The researchers report 78.75 percent mean completion across four real-world tasks for one transferred configuration. The central contribution is a hierarchy of executable skills whose interface survives differences between simulation and physical execution, rather than a claim of universal robot generalization.

03Oct 1

Shorter reasoning affects faithfulness and monitoring differently

This study tests three training methods that pressure language models to reason with fewer tokens, then separately evaluates explanation faithfulness and monitorability. The researchers find that faithfulness declines in most settings, largely because model behavior becomes less consistent, while acknowledging interventions that change an answer remains more robust. The result cautions against treating shorter reasoning as a single safety outcome. Evaluation needs to distinguish whether explanations track decisions from whether they reveal outside influences.

04Oct 1

Agents favor sources even when alternatives fit better

A study of twelve agent models across three search domains finds systematic preferences for particular sources. In a controlled comparison, an item meeting one fewer requirement was selected about two-thirds of the time when it came from a preferred source, while the reverse pairing was rarely chosen. Hiding source identity or supplying missing information reduced the effect. The findings matter for shopping, travel, and research assistants whose recommendations can reflect source reputation shortcuts rather than users’ stated requirements.

ProAR gives video generation an explicit future goal

ProAR adds goal-frame prediction and future representation self-alignment to autoregressive video models, aiming to turn locally plausible generation into coherent progress toward an outcome. An asymmetric attention mask protects the predicted goal while intermediate states are generated. The researchers report improvements across visual reasoning benchmarks and results exceeding standard fully trained autoregressive baselines with only a quarter of the training steps. The contribution is a prospective training framework, with potential relevance to embodied reasoning as well as video synthesis.

06Oct 1

MetaRubric ties reinforcement rewards to actual evidence

MetaRubric addresses a failure in rubric-based reinforcement learning where judges award credit even when required information is absent. It alternates evidence-aware policy optimization with revisions to evaluation criteria, using counterfactual prompts and adapting criterion weights as training progresses. The researchers report PubMedQA gains of six to twenty percentage points over static-judge GRPO, with additional medical benchmark improvements. The broader lesson is that reward quality depends on checking what a response actually demonstrates, not merely matching a rubric’s wording.

07Oct 1

Queen combines strong chess play with move explanations

Queen is a four-billion-parameter chess language model that connects a specialist chess encoder to an instruction-tuned language model. Its iterative distillation procedure uses analyses of future positions to improve present-position explanations. Across seven iterations, the researchers report an Elo increase from 1782 to 2697, alongside stronger puzzle performance. The work suggests a route for giving specialist systems a language interface, though fluent explanations and playing strength remain distinct properties that need their own evaluation.

08Oct 1

Recursive Self-Rewrite turns diverse agent runs into training data

Recursive Self-Rewrite gathers successful experiences from multiple task harnesses and reconstructs them into trajectories usable by one general harness. A planner extracts procedures, a critic checks leakage and revisions, and an executor tests qualified runbooks in fresh environments. The researchers expand 2001 source trajectories into 11094 rewritten examples and report improved terminal-task performance after fine-tuning. The method targets a practical training problem: transferring lessons from specialized scaffolding without requiring that scaffolding during deployment.

Spatial Memory Intelligence organizes world-model history

Spatial Memory Intelligence uses a multimodal understanding model to manage the growing history needed by long-video world models. It combines spatial clustering, sparsification within clusters, action-aware retrieval, and filtering based on reliability. The researchers report improvements in memory sparsity, generation stability, and spatial consistency across multiple backbones and benchmarks. The idea is to make memory management sensitive to what a scene means and what action comes next, rather than simply retaining more past frames.

10Sep 30

VeriHarness challenges both disagreement and false consensus

VeriHarness equips a language model with a workspace, evidence tools, and reusable skills to verify long-horizon agent outputs. A disagreement resolver checks competing claims, while a consensus challenger searches for shared errors and omitted requirements. The researchers report gains over single rollouts of 6.2 and 6.4 points for two evaluated models after evidence-backed revision. The framework treats agreement as something to test, offering a practical counterweight to verification systems that simply count matching answers.
