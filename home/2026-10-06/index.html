FLaRe studies what makes latent reasoning useful and efficient

FLaRe studies flow matching in a learned latent space for reasoning without verbalizing every intermediate step. The authors assess usefulness, diversity, explainability, refinement with additional compute, and efficiency, then propose training choices aimed at meeting all five requirements. On arithmetic benchmarks, they report reaching 97% of explicit chain-of-thought accuracy at one quarter of its latency. These results concern the evaluated tasks and comparisons, but they offer a concrete framework for testing whether hidden computation contributes meaningful reasoning.

RealtimeWAM combines one-step actions with asynchronous inference

RealtimeWAM targets two latency bottlenecks in world action models: repeated action denoising and waiting between video and action experts. It uses teacher-anchored consistency distillation for one-step actions and blockwise sharing of the video key-value cache to overlap expert execution. The authors report roughly 25-fold end-to-end speedup on an H100 with less than a one-percent performance drop across their evaluated benchmarks. The contribution is a coordinated inference design whose gains remain tied to the tested models and hardware.

Agentic retrieval improves relevance at a steep inference cost

A study of agentic retrieval combines language-model reasoning with retrievers in a ReAct loop and reports an 8.7-point nDCG@10 improvement using the same embedding model. The pipeline also generalizes across the ViDoRe v3 and BRIGHT evaluations. The cost is substantial: average retrieval time rises from 0.67 to 107.4 seconds, with 764,100 input tokens and 5,800 output tokens per query. The result makes cost-aware routing important when deciding which searches justify a reasoning agent.

HLA-WM improves long video memory without additional training

HLA-WM addresses forgetting in video world models that compress history into recurrent linear-attention states. Its training-free method stores compact transition summaries, retrieves relevant historical chunks using camera geometry, and reconstructs query-specific states. On a 60-second benchmark, the authors report better revisit consistency and camera control, with twelve times less historical-state memory than full key-value caching and at most a 1.6% throughput reduction. The work connects selective retrieval with efficient recurrent memory for longer scene rollouts.

MemAdapter tackles sycophancy induced by persistent memory

MemAdapter examines how persistent agent memories can encourage agreement with a user's historical beliefs even when current evidence points elsewhere. Self-generated feedback can destabilize test-time training

Researchers find that retaining updates from a model's own generated text can worsen predictions on independent human-written text over long streams. Matched experiments separate degraded input effects from damage stored in model weights, while frozen generation removes most measured damage in two smaller configurations. The study argues for external evidence when accepting adaptation, with conclusions grounded in the particular model configurations and update mechanisms tested.

UndoBench exposes the gap between task success and safe recovery

UndoBench separates ordinary task completion from recovery after operational faults in tool-using agents. In its lost-acknowledgment study, nominal competence reached 83.54%, while conditional recovery success fell to 46.72%; naive retries produced duplicate external effects in 53.33% of trials. The benchmark uses paired workflows and effect histories to locate failures around mutation boundaries. Results support evaluating recovery explicitly and show why verification and server-side idempotency matter when an action may have committed before its acknowledgment was lost.
