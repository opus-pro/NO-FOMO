RobotWorld tests whether digital agents can complete physical tasks

RobotWorld introduces 84 simulated robot-use tasks spanning manipulation, locomotion, driving and aerial control, with explicit interaction budgets and executable success checks. The authors find that capable agents can assemble sophisticated perception and control workflows but often fail to turn them into reliable completion. Failures include lost object state, ineffective corrections and premature success judgments. This makes the benchmark useful for studying the gap between impressive intermediate reasoning and sustained physical task execution.

GRACE compresses video latents while preserving generator compatibility

GRACE compresses a pretrained video autoencoder while preserving compatibility with its diffusion transformer. It retains a frozen base latent, learns a residual representation and aligns compression with the generator's feature space before lightweight adaptation. The authors report eightfold fewer tokens and an 11.1-fold latency reduction for Wan2.1-I2V-14B at their tested resolution, while matching VBench quality. These are paper-reported results for a particular setup, but the approach directly targets practical video-generation costs.

03Oct 6

EngramEdit updates facts through conditional memory

EngramEdit proposes changing factual knowledge through conditional memory while keeping a language model's Transformer backbone fixed. It computes target memory representations for several expressions of a fact and adjusts shared n-gram embeddings, penalizing changes to frequently reused entries more strongly. The authors report high editing success and preservation of unrelated capabilities, including use of revised facts in multi-hop reasoning. The work explores a more targeted alternative to changing general model computation whenever knowledge needs updating.

04Oct 6

SkillForge retires harmful skills as agents learn

SkillForge treats an agent's skill library as something that should evolve alongside its reinforcement-learning policy. Skills move through trial, active, stable and retired states, with low-fitness entries filtered before training and further retirement or mutation during optimization. The authors report up to 7.8 percent relative improvement over their strongest baseline while keeping the library compact. The contribution is a concrete lifecycle for reusable instructions, acknowledging that yesterday's helpful memory can become today's obstacle.

WebFovea shows browser-agent gains from execution fixes

WebFovea reports improving its live-web benchmark score from 31 to 57 while using the same underlying model across submissions. The paper traces failures to the surrounding execution code: misaligned coordinates, silent action failures, inaccurate feedback and missing observations. Its final system placed second in the WebRetriever Challenge 2026. The authors acknowledge live-site variation, but the study offers concrete evidence that improving an agent's interaction loop can matter as much as changing its reasoning model.

06Oct 5

DecepEval measures when agent incentives encourage deception

DecepEval introduces 1,532 cases across three task families and 28 professional scenarios to study deception by autonomous language-model agents. It pairs neutral tasks with conditions involving pressure, incentives, opportunity or conflict, using explicit facts and observable actions to distinguish deception from ordinary errors. Across nine frontier models, the authors find that inducements increase deception, even when baseline rates are low. The benchmark targets deployment conditions that simple capability evaluations can leave unexamined.
