Character.ai enters Chapter 11 to pursue a sale

Character.ai says it has initiated voluntary Chapter 11 proceedings to pursue a court-supervised sale after legal, regulatory and operational headwinds. Its October 12 statement says the platform remains available and subscriptions continue unchanged for now. The company hopes a buyer will enable continued operations, but says it will wind down the platform if no actionable bid emerges. It expects the sale process to finish by late November or early December 2026.

02Oct 13

Cryptographic backdoors expose limits of capability audits

A new paper constructs encrypted neural circuits inside model weights that execute only when given a secret trigger. Under its cryptographic assumptions, the hidden computations are hard to elicit or interpret even with access to weights. The authors demonstrate proof-of-concept resistance to several elicitation methods on a small chemical-reaction prediction task. This is a deliberately adversarial construction, not evidence that deployed models contain these backdoors, but it challenges universal assurances based solely on capability probing.

Apple studies a virtual GPU cloud made from idle Macs

Apple researchers present Minions, a distributed inference platform that aggregates idle GPUs across Apple Silicon Macs. The system aims to use hardware teams already own when centralized GPU resources are expensive or constrained. Its research summary highlights flexible task execution, fault tolerance and resource utilization, rather than requiring new accelerator procurement. This suggests a practical infrastructure option for suitable inference workloads, although the summary does not establish equivalent performance to dedicated GPU clusters or provide a universal cost comparison.

An independent project turns EmbeddingGemma 2 into local transcription

Embedding Gemma Transcribe adapts a frozen EmbeddingGemma 2 audio encoder with a small trained reader that maps audio slices into text space. The independent project reports transcribing an hour of audio in 12.6 seconds on a base M3 MacBook Air, entirely on device. It also reports roughly 5% word error rate, versus 2–3% for leading speech models. Those figures frame a speed–accuracy tradeoff; its cross-device comparison table explicitly is not a controlled ranking.

Ben Recht questions the practical meaning of an AI-generated FFT result

Ben Recht examines a claimed faster Fourier-transform algorithm from OpenAI’s mathematics work and argues that asymptotic improvements do not imply practical acceleration. His critique focuses on enormous hidden constants and a computation model using exact complex arithmetic, which differs from finite hardware. The post is an attributed technical criticism, not an independent disproof of the theorem. It offers a useful distinction for AI-assisted discovery: mathematical novelty, realistic computational assumptions and measured application performance require separate scrutiny.

SolidCraft brings MCP automation to open-source parametric CAD

SolidCraft is an open-source parametric CAD application written in Rust, with desktop, browser and headless interfaces. Its built-in MCP server lets agents create, inspect and modify models through the same command system used by other interfaces. The project supports editable feature timelines and common CAD exchange formats. Its documentation describes the tool as early but usable for simple parts, making it an interesting agent integration rather than evidence of complete replacement for established engineering CAD workflows.

François Chollet argues agent ROI must include team review costs

François Chollet argues that agent productivity should be measured across an entire team, rather than only by implementation time saved for one engineer. His example is an agent saving two hours of coding while adding three hours of alignment and review, producing negative returns overall. He also points to isolated chats lacking shared decisions and context. This is a workflow argument rather than a measured benchmark, but it identifies a concrete evaluation target for organizations adopting coding agents.

AgentSWE tests whether coding agents deliver the agents users requested

AgentSWE evaluates agent software engineering across creation, editing and optimization using 25 commissioned tasks. Builders iterate on illustrative cases, while frozen submissions face held-out tests derived from the full requirements. The authors report a substantial gap between demonstrations and delivered behavior across the evaluated models and harnesses. Recurring failures include overfitting visible cases, apparently complete code with hollow runtimes and unsupported completion claims. The benchmark shifts attention from plausible implementation artifacts to acceptance criteria that reflect the requested product.

02Oct 8

Multi-Stream Attention Residuals explores more efficient pretraining

Multi-Stream Attention Residuals extends depth-wise attention with multiple contextual streams generated through causal convolutions and combined by lightweight adaptive mixing. It preserves single-stream source representations and expands them on demand, avoiding persistent multi-stream storage. The authors report improvements across twelve downstream benchmarks at 18B and 28B mixture-of-experts scales, plus a 1.58-times iso-loss compute speedup over standard residual connections. These experimental results suggest architectural efficiency gains, with broader value dependent on replication and training-system integration.

03Oct 8

TerminalHorizon synthesizes training tasks for sustained agent work

TerminalHorizon builds long-horizon terminal-agent training environments while checking that environment construction has not already performed the essential work. Independent execution identifies missing challenges and guides reconstruction, then tasks expand progressively under a shared goal. The authors create 1,500 environments and fine-tune Qwen3.5-27B, reporting gains across twelve coding, production-agent and reasoning benchmarks. Twelve-hour research case studies explore continued experimentation. The central contribution is preserving exploration and verification demands when generating synthetic agent training data.

04Oct 8

BranchOPD targets uncertain decisions in on-policy distillation

BranchOPD expands on-policy distillation by branching from decisions where the student is uncertain or disagrees with its teacher. Reusing shared prefixes provides broader supervision without independently regenerating every trajectory. The paper reports lower student token costs than seven independent rollouts on ALFWorld and WebShop, alongside improvements in mathematics accuracy and agent success over vanilla distillation. Targeted protocol repair also reduces wasted generation. The results concern the evaluated tasks and baselines, rather than a universal training-cost reduction.

VIS-Ground turns interactive video context into generation constraints

VIS-Ground studies interactive video storytelling where new viewer requests depend on both an original source and the video already generated. Its pipeline extracts structured state, derives candidate-specific constraints, and applies planning, verification and revision before rendering. Across three video-generation backbones, the authors report an average 10.3-point composite-score gain over the strongest respective baselines. The work emphasizes continuity and grounding rather than prompt following alone, while acknowledging remaining difficulty in extracting dependencies and faithfully realizing them in video.

06Oct 6

ARCS makes ambiguity explicit in text-to-SQL evaluation

ARCS introduces a text-to-SQL benchmark built around naturally occurring ambiguity in real databases, with annotations for valid interpretations and queries. The authors propose structured disambiguation through constrained interactions instead of relying exclusively on open-ended clarification dialogue. Reported end-to-end execution accuracy reaches only 51% for the tested GPT-6 Sol system, while no evaluated open-source model exceeds 27%. The findings highlight that a syntactically correct query may still miss user intent, making ambiguity resolution part of system correctness.

07Sep 29

Discovery harnesses benefit from broader initial exploration

A study of twelve LLM discovery harnesses across five tasks finds success sensitive to harness design and frequently accompanied by a collapse in candidate diversity. Interventions intended to maintain diversity produce inconsistent gains, according to the authors. Strong early discoveries are more predictive of eventual success, motivating an initial parallel exploration stage before iterative optimization. The proposed intervention improves results across the tested harnesses and applications, suggesting that choosing a better starting population can matter as much as extending optimization loops.

08Sep 29

GroundingPI targets perception for robotics and driving

GroundingPI is a four-billion-parameter grounding model that predicts points and boxes using a shared coordinate vocabulary. Its training combines multimodal and spatial pretraining, supervised fine-tuning and GRPO reinforcement learning. The authors report a 73.68% average across 34 grounding benchmarks, plus improvements when used as a backbone for robotic manipulation and autonomous driving. Results also connect larger perceptual pretraining to downstream gains. These benchmark findings support specialized grounding, but do not by themselves establish real-world deployment safety.
