SYNC shares facts across models through symbolic indices

The SYNC paper proposes communication between heterogeneous language models using shared word indices and receiver-local activation directions, without fine-tuning or learned translators. Across controlled scene tasks, the authors report strong selective fact use across multiple model families, while a harder landmark task remains below text communication. The result offers a concrete alternative to exchanging architecture-dependent hidden vectors. Its evidence concerns structured experimental tasks, leaving general conversational and production-agent benefits to be established.

Emotion benchmark tests agents beyond cooperative users

Tau-Emotion-Bench evaluates tool-calling agents across ten domains while varying user emotion and preserving the underlying task. Its Tracer pipeline generates grounded tasks and trajectories, allowing dissatisfied simulated users to end inefficient interactions. 03Oct 13

CALIF addresses reward hacking in instruction-following RL

Researchers show that verifiable instruction-following rewards can favor outputs that satisfy formatting constraints while failing to answer the actual request. Their CALIF method adds a calibrated likelihood bonus from the existing frozen reference policy, without requiring a separate judge or learned reward model. Experiments across models from 1.7 to 8 billion parameters report better constraint satisfaction with preserved response quality. The result illustrates why verifier success alone is an incomplete training objective.

Glance Forcing reduces video distillation trajectory costs

Glance Forcing splits video diffusion distillation into complementary slow and fast experts to handle different denoising regimes. The authors report that one probability-flow trajectory can outperform their fully trained baseline, reducing trajectory collection to roughly one A100 GPU-hour while slightly improving the reported VBench total score. The striking efficiency claim applies to trajectory collection, not necessarily the entire training pipeline. The paper also reports gains in motion and human preference within its evaluations.

Doc2Harness finds persistent gaps in agent harness engineering

Doc2Harness evaluates whether language models can build complete coding-agent harnesses from interface contracts and development documentation. Candidate harnesses are tested separately using scripted model behavior and observable outputs. Across nineteen models and 328 test cases, the paper reports a best pass rate of 53.05 percent, achieved by GPT-6 Astra. Even the strongest result leaves substantial failures, emphasizing that reliable agent infrastructure requires engineering beyond choosing a capable underlying model.

ATAS uses world-state memory for consistent generated video

Any Time, Any Space organizes agentic video generation around explicit scene geometry and time-indexed event memory. It converts screenplays into shot requests, retrieves relevant evidence, and admits generated observations to memory only after checks. Across four generators, the authors report improved cross-shot consistency and stronger aggregate benchmark results. The approach addresses revisiting locations and replaying events, suggesting that persistent state management can matter as much as individual clip quality for longer productions.
