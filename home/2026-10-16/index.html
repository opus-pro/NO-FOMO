01Oct 15

MRTPO assigns each RL reward to the tokens it evaluates

MRTPO proposes more precise credit assignment when language models optimize several verifiable rewards at once. Instead of spreading each reward across an entire response, it uses the information inspected by that reward's verifier to estimate token relevance. The method compares distributions with and without verifier information, requiring neither auxiliary models nor token labels. The authors report faster convergence and better results on text to SQL, instruction constraints, and tool calling than their comparison methods.

02Oct 14

SpecAgent makes strong models review grounded tool trajectories

SpecAgent uses a smaller model to draft sequences of tool operations and a stronger model to review them together. The draft model may execute read only calls, but holds actions that would change the environment for verification. The stronger model accepts the valid prefix and repairs the first rejected step. The authors report near strong model success on one benchmark while roughly halving wall time and memory traffic, suggesting a practical efficiency route for tool agents.

03Oct 14

Mosaic trains agents across compaction and subagent sessions

Mosaic addresses the mismatch between single session reinforcement learning and agents that compact context or fork subagents. Its hierarchical optimization accounts for session usage at the trajectory level and estimates each session's marginal contribution when assigning advantages. In BrowseComp experiments with Qwen3.5 4B, the authors report better performance than broadcasting the same trajectory advantage to every session, while using fewer sessions. The work treats context management and delegation as trainable resource allocation decisions.

06Oct 14

Speculative Forcing skips unnecessary video denoising

Speculative Forcing reduces video generation work by accepting individual tokens before every denoising step finishes. Accepted tokens remain available as context while later computation focuses on tokens needing more refinement. The paper studies both a training free verifier and a learned verifier. Its authors report more than 1.5 times speedup on LongLive with similar quality, and 1.35 times on Lingbot World 2.0, pointing toward more responsive streaming and interactive video generation.

Orbia measures whether generated video worlds remain consistent

Orbia introduces a benchmark for three dimensional consistency in interactive video world models. Its 720 instances and six exploration and revisit protocols evaluate input preservation, persistence of newly generated content, and consistency across viewpoints. The study covers 27 models using their native interaction interfaces. By checking geometry and revisits as well as appearance, the benchmark targets a gap between visually convincing frames and a world that stays coherent when an observer moves through it.
