Anthropic proposes a growing public-benefit equity fund

Anthropic says it will commit 5% of its shares to a wholly owned Public Benefit Fund, subject to stockholder approval. The Verge reports that security researchers uncovered an operator using AI agents to compromise more than 100 websites, with Reuters identifying John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins among affected organizations. 01Oct 15

HexaShell tests coding agents as robot controllers

HexaShell uses a thin harness to translate a vision-language coding agent’s commands into robot motion. The agent learns reusable tools from failed attempts and uses calibrated camera observations for geometry. The paper reports 39.7% success across 42 RoboDojo tasks, with much stronger pushing than tight insertion. Its findings locate a major limitation in contact-rich execution, showing that recognizing a failure and reasoning about it do not automatically produce a successful physical maneuver.

02Oct 15

PPMem gives video world models compact planar memory

PPMem augments a video diffusion transformer with persistent three-dimensional memory represented by planes. Each generated chunk jointly predicts video and geometry, then updates memory that conditions later chunks through rendered depth. The paper reports a roughly five-megabyte full-scene representation and stronger video quality on two datasets. Its appeal is explicit, compact scene structure that can preserve consistency across viewpoint changes while also producing useful depth, normals and exportable surfaces.

03Oct 14

Whole-file selection beats compression in a coding-agent study

A study of 300 SWE-rebench issues compares context strategies under equal per-issue token budgets. Its CQ2-CARE selector preserves chosen files verbatim and reports better resolution rates than tested compressors and retrievers with two models. The authors attribute failures to missing edit targets or altered source text that prevents patches from applying. The result argues for measuring context usefulness by successful repository edits, rather than treating maximum compression as the primary objective.

04Oct 14

OS-Helix trains coordinated GUI and code execution

OS-Helix trains a computer-using agent to coordinate graphical actions and code execution within a shared action space. The authors report that simply adding code tools can degrade an existing GUI agent, motivating hybrid supervision and reinforcement learning. Their step-balanced optimization prevents long code actions from dominating the training objective. The strongest reported variant reaches 66.44% success on OSWorld-Verified, making coordination policy an important part of the system rather than an incidental tool choice.

05Oct 13

BizBench checks whether business agents obey constraints along the way

BizBench evaluates long-horizon agents against both final goals and valid intermediate execution in simulated business procedures. Its environment contains 789 documents, 601 discoverable tools and 50 tasks involving constraints, dependencies and conflicting guidance. The paper reports strict success rates of 64.0% and 61.3% for its two leading models. The gap between goal attainment and valid execution shows why a seemingly correct outcome may conceal costly process violations in business automation.

06Oct 13

Speculative sampling explores responsive video generation with larger windows

A new paper proposes speculative sampling for interactive video generation, combining larger bidirectional modeling windows with incremental delivery. It appends noise latents, caches predictions while conditions remain unchanged and leaves the uncommitted future replaceable. The authors argue that this removes a waiting-time bound imposed by chunk boundaries. The approach reframes the quality-latency tradeoff around sampling speed and reusable predictions, although the abstract does not establish a universal production performance improvement.

07Oct 13

CarryOn organizes long-running agent context around reusable task state

CarryOn replaces repeated processing of interaction history with reusable task objects, execution context and a short tail of recent interactions. It updates affected records against evidence and environment checks while preserving unaffected state. The paper reports better results on OSWorld 2.0 and BrowseComp-Plus alongside token reductions of 51.9% and 22.9%. The central idea is to preserve actionable progress directly, reducing the cost of reconstructing it from a compressed narrative.
