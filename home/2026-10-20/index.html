DeepSeek releases DeepInfer, its production inference framework

DeepSeek has released DeepInfer under Apache 2.0, exposing the inference framework behind its large-scale production services. The release supports DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, optimized kernels, asynchronous scheduling, speculative decoding and structured outputs on NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. It currently provides offline inference: a complete service still needs a KV cache system, disaggregation and load balancing. That distinction matters for teams assessing how much deployment engineering remains.

03Oct 20

Google opens TorchTPU for native PyTorch workloads on TPUs

Google’s TorchTPU repository provides a PyTorch backend for Tensor Processing Units, with custom ATen kernels, a compilation cache and torch.compile integration. It includes distributed-training support and examples spanning models such as Llama, Qwen and ResNet. The release gives PyTorch users another route to TPU execution while retaining familiar model code. Actual portability and performance depend on operation coverage and workload characteristics, so the examples are starting points for application-specific evaluation.

01Oct 18

AdaExploreBench tests agents when hidden rules change

AdaExploreBench evaluates whether language-model agents can discover hidden environmental rules and recognize when those rules drift without warning. Across eight models, the authors report persistent difficulty with proactive exploration; GPT-6 Sol reaches 61.9% calibrated accuracy. Supplying sufficient evidence or removing drift improves average performance by roughly ten points. The result separates reasoning over available information from the harder operational problem of gathering the right evidence and noticing when it has become stale.

Fréchet Forcing extends world-model rollouts beyond short training clips

Fréchet Forcing fine-tunes streaming world models using real-data statistics and action-conditioned objectives rather than a long-rollout teacher. The authors report that MiniWorld-1B, pretrained on 253-frame clips, can remain stable beyond 4,000 frames after at most 36 GPU-hours of fine-tuning on 381-frame clips. The method targets accumulated errors when generated history feeds future predictions. Its reported results cover robotic manipulation and camera-controlled exploration, rather than every form of interactive video.

03Oct 18

TokenWorth learns which agent memories justify their cost

TokenWorth treats agent memory as a constrained resource and learns which experiences are worth retaining and retrieving from sparse execution feedback. The authors find that sophisticated memory systems can lose their advantage under simultaneous generation, interaction and storage limits. Across three LifelongAgentBench environments, their method reportedly achieves the best success rate with the fewest output tokens among evaluated approaches. The practical contribution is selecting useful memory rather than assuming that more stored experience necessarily helps.

05Oct 18

Stateful Harness separates world rules from generated video

Stateful Harness maintains explicit world state and resolves actions before asking a frozen video model to render the resulting events. Its WorldState-Bench uses 50 counterfactual pairs with identical starting images and actions but different hidden states. For MiniMax-H3, the authors report required endings in 92% of pairs with the full harness, compared with 32% under rule-only prompting. The result supports testing persistent rules separately from the visual plausibility of generated worlds.

OpenVLHarness combines visual tools with persistent multimodal evidence

OpenVLHarness connects visual reasoning models to heterogeneous tools through a capability layer and persistent multimodal memory. Images, structured data and web references remain addressable while generated code combines tool results. Across 22 benchmarks and eight backbones, the authors report average improvements of 9.7 to 12.6 points over matched no-tool baselines. The work argues that better access to usable evidence can matter more than simply increasing a model’s internal reasoning budget.

07Oct 17

Study finds safety scores can reflect labeling policy more than capability

A guardrail study examines models trained on the same prompts under two different definitions of unsafe content. After addressing benchmark contamination, the authors find that some safety scores vary sharply with labeling policy despite shared architectures and training machinery. They also show that a small parameter update can move selected benchmark scores across their range. The paper argues for reporting policy definitions, contamination and seed variability alongside aggregate safety metrics before comparing systems.

08Oct 18

InfiniDream generates editable procedural 3D worlds

InfiniDream uses vision-language models to construct procedural 3D assets and assemble them into large scenes. Symbolic constraints reuse existing generation algorithms while preserving editability and seed-based variation; scene planning and guided placement organize the resulting worlds. The authors evaluate 100 object tasks and 20 scene tasks, reporting improvements across four perceptual metrics and human-preference checks. The approach is relevant to synthetic data and game development where reusable procedural structure matters alongside visual appearance.
