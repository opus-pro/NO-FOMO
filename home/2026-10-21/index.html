HarnessAdapt-RL trains the policy and its agent harness together

HarnessAdapt-RL adapts a coding agent’s harness while training its policy, using teacher-generated skills and evolving tools and hooks. With a 9B model, the authors report pass-at-one gains of 9.8, 12.6, and 6.4 percentage points over GRPO on three SWE-bench variants. Some learned benefits survive removing skills, but removing evolved tools hurts performance. The result argues for evaluating the deployed model and harness as a coupled system.

02Oct 19

Self-Credit makes sparse agent rewards more informative

Self-Credit uses an agent’s own citations to earlier decisive actions as a starting point for reward credit assignment. It compares these signals across successful and failed rollouts, then reweights training advantages. The authors report improvements over their strongest agentic reinforcement-learning baselines on BrowseComp-Plus, GAIA, and xBench-DeepSearch. The method addresses a recurring problem in long tasks: a final success signal alone says little about which intermediate actions helped.

04Oct 18

Anti-Persistence questions storing agent-generated rules forever

The Anti-Persistence paper argues that stored agent-generated rules can reproduce errors through repeated cycles of abstraction and execution. Its alternative retains trajectories and outcomes but reconstructs task-specific guidance when needed, then discards that guidance after use. The authors report higher held-out success and lower average model-call cost than the evaluated memory designs. The finding suggests that accumulating experience and permanently preserving inferred rules should be treated as separate design choices.
