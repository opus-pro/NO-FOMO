01Oct 20

PACE compresses recurrent model state with less frequent rounding

PACE proposes post-hoc four-bit quantization for recurrent states in linear-attention models. It rounds states periodically rather than after every token, buffers intervening tokens, aligns scales with columns, and keeps selected error-sensitive heads at eight bits. Across four hybrid models, the authors report competitive accuracy with an eight-bit baseline on the three larger models at roughly two thirds of its memory. The approach targets concurrent decoding capacity without requiring model fine-tuning.

02Oct 20

ALLOT allocates video attention sparsity by output impact

ALLOT is a training-free method for allocating block-sparse attention across video diffusion layers and denoising steps. It probes each layer-step pair’s contribution to final-video error, then optimizes density under a measured latency budget while preserving existing kernels. The authors report an 11.8 dB PSNR gain on HunyuanVideo with XAttention and favorable results across nine settings. The contribution concerns where sparsity is applied, rather than inventing a replacement attention kernel.

Harness Scaling trains agents across execution configurations

Harness Scaling treats diversity in agent execution environments as a dimension of reinforcement learning. Instead of training through one fixed harness, it samples a structured space of configurations with balanced coverage and controlled variation. The authors report improved generalization to unseen configurations and transfer to external black-box harnesses across multiple benchmarks and model scales. The practical implication is that tool and context-management arrangements can be part of training diversity, not merely deployment plumbing.

StreamEval tests whether interactive video follows instructions

StreamEval evaluates interactive streaming video across avatar dialogue, performance, world generation, instruction editing, and reference editing. Its 300 scripted tests span 15 to 300 seconds, measuring execution, state consistency, visual fidelity, and audiovisual coordination across 37 models. The authors find that attractive output and identity retention do not guarantee reliable instruction following. Low leading scores on several execution metrics point to timing and preservation constraints as unresolved obstacles for interactive generative systems.

05Oct 19

Cache Engineering gives agents direct control of KV state

Cache Engineering proposes letting agents drop cache entries and reposition retained keys without rewriting their trajectory or re-prefilling remaining context. Its AKV serving engine handles the resulting history-dependent reuse, eviction, and restoration. The authors report a 1.7-point average score improvement and 27.7 percent lower cost than threshold-triggered summarization, with additional serving gains. The work makes cache policy an explicit agent decision and shows why token-prefix matching alone can become insufficient.
