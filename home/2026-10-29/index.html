TAO schedules agent inference around tool readiness

TAO proposes joint scheduling for agentic services that alternate between GPU model inference and external CPU tools. Its scheduling interface combines near-term tool readiness, time-decayed cache credit, and token footprint, then jointly selects and places sessions under capacity constraints. Across 12 reported settings, the authors claim up to 26.8 percent lower latency and 29.5 percent higher decode throughput against the best evaluated baselines. The gains are workload-specific experimental results.

03Oct 25

DTCA assigns token-level RL credit in diffusion language models

DTCA addresses reinforcement-learning credit assignment in diffusion language models, where text emerges through repeated denoising rather than ordinary autoregressive decoding. It estimates token contributions from confidence changes in remaining masked positions, groups signals by part of speech, and calibrates learning signals using entropy changes. Experiments on LLaDA-8B-Instruct report gains of up to nine points on MATH500 and 5.3 on GSM8K over the base model across evaluated generation budgets.

OmniShowcase uses geometry to preserve subjects across video viewpoints

OmniShowcase targets subject-consistent video generation when camera movement reveals surfaces absent from the reference images. It combines synthetic training videos grounded in 3D assets with surface-normal turntables reconstructed from multiple views. They report improved subject identity and 3D consistency while retaining competitive video quality, making explicit geometry a promising complement to purely visual reference conditioning.

LongZero trains long-context reasoning from self-created tasks

LongZero extends reinforcement learning from zero external data to long-context reasoning. A model first creates compact questions, answers, and supporting evidence, then expands the evidence into long multi-document contexts. Training combines self-written multi-hop reasoning and multi-round retrieval tasks with GRPO. RealCLI measures whether coding agents can collaborate

RealCLI pairs fully specified command-line tasks with scenarios where users progressively introduce information, corrections, constraint changes, and authorization decisions. Its evaluation framework covers five dimensions, 22 subdimensions, and 51 interaction templates. The reported best average score reaches only 49.2 percent, while the peak joint collaboration score is 37.6 percent. The results distinguish completing a task from collaborating effectively, highlighting weaknesses in steerability, trust calibration, and respecting safety boundaries during execution.

08Sep 29

Banking-agent benchmark checks required steps as well as outcomes

TauIndianBankBench evaluates 1,000 Indian retail-banking tasks by checking both final records and the required actions taken during a conversation. The authors find that outcome-only evaluation incorrectly counts 6.5 to 12 percent of sessions as successful when agents skip required steps or make unrequested changes. This matters for tool-using assistants because some mandatory procedures leave no database trace. Reliable evaluation must therefore inspect the process as well as the final state.
