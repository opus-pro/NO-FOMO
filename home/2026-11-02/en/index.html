LARC moves image-generation reasoning into continuous latent space

LARC addresses a mismatch between discrete language reasoning and continuous image generation by reasoning over hidden states. The proposed training process first converts token-based reasoning through curriculum supervised fine-tuning, then applies information-gain reinforcement learning without ground-truth supervision. The authors report improved prompt following and compositional fidelity, including a seven-percentage-point GenEval gain over BAGEL. The work suggests a route to test-time scaling for image generation, although the reported gains remain specific to the paper's evaluated models and benchmarks.

RoboArchitect improves frozen robot policies by evolving their harness

RoboArchitect uses a coding model to inspect robot rollouts, diagnose failures, and revise the harness around a frozen policy. The authors report unseen-scene success improving from 9.7% to 43.3% and unseen-task success from 12.7% to 40.2% across their evaluations, without updating policy weights. The harness can develop persistent memory and practical control strategies. The result points to orchestration and tool-use logic as substantial sources of robotics improvement, alongside the more familiar route of policy fine-tuning.

rTaste questions whether research agents choose their best experiments

rTaste evaluates how six frontier models propose and prioritize experiments based on seven NLP papers. Of 415 proposed minimum viable experiments, auditors judged 194 sound, valuable, and new, but only 73 also extended beyond the seed paper in a non-obvious way. Agent rankings beat random selection but were not shown to improve on choosing the first proposal. Teams cost more without clear overall quality gains, highlighting a gap between generating worthwhile ideas and reliably deciding which deserve execution.

CodeSpecWeave synthesizes software tasks whose requirements interact, such as interface compatibility and error handling, rather than treating each constraint as an isolated checklist item. HALO finds excessive intervention is a major proactive-agent failure

HALO evaluates whether proactive agents intervene at the right time and with appropriate scope. Its human-annotated dataset contains 8,000 responses from ten models across 400 scenarios. The best proprietary and open models reached 72.88% and 56.63% acceptability, respectively. Premature timing or excessive intensity accounted for 61.20% of rejected responses. The findings make restraint an explicit evaluation target: retrieving evidence and recognizing a possible need are not sufficient to justify taking stronger action.

CaPtain trains a small open model to control robots through code

CaPtain applies supervised fine-tuning followed by multi-turn reinforcement learning to code-as-policy robot agents. Starting from Qwen3.5-4B, the authors report held-out LIBERO success rising from zero-shot failure to 55.4% after supervised training and 73.8% after reinforcement learning. A physical critic uses privileged simulator state during training to assign credit across requests, while the deployed policy does not receive that state. The release includes code, data, and weights, supporting investigation of smaller trainable robotic agents.

SimpleLoRIF traces safety degradation to apparently benign training data

SimpleLoRIF uses scalable gradient embeddings to identify fine-tuning examples associated with unsafe behavior, including examples that ordinary content filters might consider benign. The authors build SafeTulu by removing the 20% of Tulu 3 examples most aligned with unsafe responses. They report reducing GCG attack success by up to 22% across evaluated model families while largely preserving capabilities. The work connects post-training data selection with safety outcomes and releases resources for studying different filtering fractions and subsets.
