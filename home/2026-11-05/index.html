01Nov 4

Agentic RL gains depend on the available turn budget

A study of multi-turn reinforcement learning finds that agent gains concentrate in error recovery and long-horizon resilience, rather than uniformly better single-step decisions. Advantages shrink when interaction budgets tighten, creating a mismatch between unconstrained evaluations and constrained deployments. The authors propose BudgetMix RL, which varies interaction budgets during training, and report more robust performance across execution settings. The result suggests evaluating agents jointly on task success and allowed turns instead of treating a single benchmark score as portable.

02Nov 4

LiveFoley targets streaming video-to-audio with low latency

LiveFoley proposes a streaming video-to-audio architecture that addresses visual look-ahead latency and the quality loss from denoising audio patches in isolation. A trailing sliding window adapts visual encoders for streaming, while a local diffusion head uses additional temporal context. The authors report alignment comparable to offline models and stronger quality than compared streaming systems. For interactive worlds, the contribution is a configurable balance among latency, compute, and audio quality rather than a single fixed operating point.

03Nov 4

NEO-VLA gives action policies access to evolving visual features

NEO-VLA replaces a fixed visual-encoder endpoint with lightweight image tokenization and joint vision-language processing throughout the backbone. A flow-matching action expert can use instruction-conditioned visual representations at multiple depths. The authors report stronger and more robust manipulation than encoder-based counterparts in controlled comparisons, arguing that action-critical details can peak before the final representation. The paper challenges a common architecture assumption in robotics: perception need not finish before action reasoning begins using its intermediate features.

04Nov 2

VideoMemBench exposes weak memory across video conversations

VideoMemBench evaluates whether multimodal models remember both visual events and their own earlier answers during extended video conversations. It includes 326 videos and 2,476 dialogue turns, with 1,387 turns depending on prior context. The authors verify that dependencies are necessary and not leaked in later questions. Among more than 20 tested models, the best reported overall score is 45.7 percent. The benchmark highlights a gap between processing long video inputs and reliably maintaining conversational memory about them.

05Nov 1

Compact reasoning histories can reduce later agent costs

This study rewrites earlier reasoning into compact state records before pending tool results arrive, then compares subsequent behavior with continuations from the original history. Across tested open-weight models, accepted compact histories usually shorten future reasoning, and a coding case study finds less repeated verification. Total spending can fall, but quality and time depend on the target model and compressor. The work supports model-specific compaction policies evaluated for retained capability, rather than assuming shorter context always improves overall execution.
