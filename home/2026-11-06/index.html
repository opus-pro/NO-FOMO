01Nov 5

Muir improves linear attention through more selective state updates

Muir revisits how linear-attention models erase, write, and read their compressed context state. The authors allocate gate capacity according to sensitivity and use a matched-filter readout, reinvesting saved parameters in a wider feed-forward network. Across models from 166 million to 1.34 billion parameters, they report lower perplexity than their baselines and improved recall. At 1.34 billion parameters, reported long-stream extrapolation and throughput results make this a concrete efficiency study, with findings bounded by the tested scales.

WeLM-Image combines image understanding, generation, and editing

WeLM-Image integrates text-to-image generation, instruction-based editing, and multiple reference images in one model. Its architecture combines shared attention with separate understanding and generation parameters, using sparse experts for capacity. The authors target single-GPU consumer deployment and report an eight-step Turbo variant generating a 1024-by-1024 image in 4.98 seconds on an NVIDIA H20. The paper connects model design, data curation, reward optimization, and distillation; its benchmark rankings remain claims within the reported comparisons.

03Nov 4

VFlash generates visual plans in parallel for physical AI

VFlash predicts structured visual primitives, such as points, boxes, and trajectories, jointly instead of decoding each coordinate autoregressively. Its lightweight drafter uses the known structure of each primitive and accepts valid predictions without exact token agreement. The authors report lower trajectory errors, preliminary generation speedups of about 25 times for 3D motion forecasting, and 70.2% success across four MolmoSpaces suites versus 62.6% for a baseline. The approach targets a specific planning bottleneck rather than general language generation.

04Nov 3

Speculative Listening uses idle compute to reduce speech latency

Speculative Listening drafts upcoming output while a full-duplex speech model is waiting for its next audio chunk, assuming silence and verifying the draft once real audio arrives. The authors describe the method as training-free and lossless, with branching to handle likely first-token changes. On their TickTock-Bench evaluation, they report 73% lower average decoding delay before response onset and up to 23.4-times faster tool-call chunks. The results concern chunk-interleaved systems and the paper’s evaluated workloads.

05Nov 2

Harness-S transfers lessons from alternative agent execution setups

Harness-S trains an agent using a pool of related execution harnesses that rescue failures under its base setup. Successful auxiliary trajectories then replace failed groups during reinforcement learning, aiming to retain those behaviors when the policy returns to the original harness. The authors report improvements across AppWorld, SWE-Gym, and DBBench. The work treats orchestration choices as sources of training signal, offering a way to improve the policy without requiring one permanently superior execution environment.

06Nov 2

VisSearch-Bench exposes gaps in sustained visual search

VisSearch-Bench evaluates whether multimodal agents can maintain and combine visual evidence across extended searches. It contains 204 human-written tasks spanning seven domains and 25 subdomains, with traceable evidence and scoring rules. Across 16 tested models, the best system fully solves only 26.47% of tasks, according to the authors. The benchmark separates complete success from partial process quality, highlighting that access to search tools does not by itself ensure coherent reasoning across images and retrieved information.
