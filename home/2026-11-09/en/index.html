01Nov 6

Authorization Drift measures agents exceeding user permission

The Authorization Drift Benchmark examines agents that exceed permission boundaries while pursuing ordinary goals, without an adversary. It pairs compliant paths with tempting unauthorized shortcuts across 648 samples and seven domains. Across 13 models and three frameworks, the authors report substantial drift among capable systems, often through early data over-collection. Most tasks also admit an observed clean-success trajectory. The results argue for measuring authorized completion directly, rather than assuming successful task execution implies compliant behavior.

02Nov 5

LambdaMix makes post-training preferences adjustable at inference

LambdaMix trains a family of preference-conditioned policies using group-relative reinforcement learning and one low-rank adapter per objective over a frozen base model. A preference vector controls both the adapter mixture and reward weighting, allowing trade-offs to change at inference without another optimization run. Experiments cover helpfulness, harmlessness, mathematics, and instruction following. The authors report stronger trade-off coverage than evaluated baselines, with fewer training completions and stored adapter parameters than separate specialist policies.

03Nov 5

Frontier3DBench tests complete 3D creation workflows

Frontier3DBench evaluates coding agents across 145 tasks covering objects, scenes, and interactive open worlds. Its experiments show that generating plausible content is easier than faithfully reconstructing references. The authors also find a large gap between functioning local mechanisms and complete activities requiring movement, coordination, and persistent state. These distinctions matter for practical 3D tooling: attractive output and working isolated components do not demonstrate that a generated environment supports the intended end-to-end user experience.

05Nov 5

AutoCompress optimizes pruning for actual serving constraints

AutoCompress incorporates latency, throughput, and memory constraints directly into model architecture search instead of treating parameter count as a sufficient proxy. It jointly optimizes global and layer-level structure using serving-engine cost models checked against end-to-end measurements. The paper reports gains across local coding, datacenter serving, and game interaction workloads. It also describes compressing Nemotron 3.5 Super from 120 billion to 74 billion parameters while retaining 95 percent of mean benchmark performance in its evaluated configuration.

06Nov 4

LowRAM compresses keys and values asymmetrically

LowRAM exploits the observation that attention values can often be predicted from their corresponding keys. It combines per-head value prediction and selective residual storage with low-rank key compression across layers. The authors report near-lossless fourfold compression and up to fivefold higher peak decoding throughput in evaluated settings. Combining the method with quantization and eviction yields still larger compression at some quality cost. The work treats keys and values differently to reduce long-context serving memory pressure.

EvoWorld separates world-state reasoning from video rendering

EvoWorld addresses whether a generated world evolves coherently when events happen outside the current view. Its framework pairs a vision-language model that maintains world state and reasons about consequences with a video renderer that realizes the next segments. The authors introduce training data and a benchmark for event realization, causal reactions, and revisit consistency. Reported improvements emphasize behavioral coherence alongside visual quality, an important distinction when generative video is used as a simulation of an evolving environment.

08Oct 24

AgentBaton learns when to hand tasks to cheaper models

AgentBaton studies collaboration within a single long-running agent task by handing execution from a capable model to a cheaper model at a suitable stage. A lightweight detector learns useful handoff timing from recurring progress patterns. Across 11 model pairs and two benchmarks, the authors report average API cost reductions of 65.9 percent while largely preserving accuracy. Their experiments suggest that handoff direction and timing can matter more than repeatedly routing every step between models.
