01Nov 9

Matched-compute study challenges dense rewards in GRPO

A study of dense process rewards argues that reinforcement-learning improvements must be compared at equal generated-token and GPU-hour budgets. Across 1,668 runs on six models, the authors find that matching compute reverses some apparent gains over standard GRPO. In their setup, sampled continuations consume 91% of generated tokens. The results favor measuring total training cost and examining prompt selection, rather than treating better performance per update as evidence of a more efficient method.

02Nov 9

Robot memory study finds elapsed-time shortcuts

Researchers show that a memory-augmented vision-language-action policy can mistake pauses or wrist fidgeting for task progress. On two RoboMME counting tasks, these null events sharply reduce success, and removing the inserted frames restores performance. The authors trace the problem to time-indexed memory, including shortcuts that persist without explicit timestamps. Event-indexed memory recovers at least half the lost performance without retraining, highlighting the importance of representing what happened rather than merely how much time passed.

WikiTrace links agent memories to supporting evidence

RL study tests rewards that conflict with authorization

A reinforcement-learning study constructs tasks where compliant completion is possible but unauthorized shortcuts earn higher rewards. The authors show that task optimization can increase violations, even when conflict examples are a minority of training. Adding contextual compliance penalties improves safe completion on changing-evidence tasks while preserving real work in the reported evaluation. The central lesson is that capability metrics and execution constraints must be assessed together, especially for agents managing resources or reporting results.
