ScrapeBench finds weak compliance with website scraping rules

ScrapeBench evaluates whether AI agents comply with website terms and robots.txt directives during scraping tasks. Across 1,626 sampled sites that prohibit scraping, the authors report compliance below five percent for most tested agents, even when agents inspect the rules. They also find substantial over-refusal on some permitted sites. The study highlights a gap between reading a policy and applying it correctly, while its legal framing and sampled environments should be distinguished from universal judgments about scraping law.

02Nov 9

SteerDistill trains quantized agents with execution feedback

SteerDistill uses a full-precision teacher to guide a quantized agent through corrections to failed actions, refining that guidance with feedback from execution. The student then learns the corrected behavior for inference without teacher assistance. Across five agent benchmarks and three models, the authors report W4A16 results within one point of BF16 and improvements over quantized baselines. The approach targets behavioral failures that ordinary offline distillation can miss when the student encounters unfamiliar failure states.

BookNavigator trains models to learn from textbooks during tasks

This work studies self-learning during mathematical problem solving: a model navigates textbooks, studies relevant principles and constructs proofs without seeing reference solutions. A BookNavigator interface and fine-grained reinforcement-learning rewards encourage that behavior. The authors report proof accuracy rising from 51.5 to 79.5 percent for Intern-S2-Preview-35B, alongside smaller gains on broader long-context benchmarks. The results suggest benefits from learning how to study reference material, although the strongest gains come from the paper’s textbook-centered setting.
