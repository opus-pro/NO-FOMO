01Nov 11

Decision models can teach better than they act

A study separates decision models' roles as actors, judges, and reinforcement-learning teachers. On ALFWorld and WebShop, the authors find that models can evaluate useful actions after observing a trajectory even when their own action choices struggle. Using JEV as a teacher raises a Qwen2.5-1.5B policy's reported success from 72.8% to 82.9% on ALFWorld and 56.8% to 71.4% on WebShop. The result suggests evaluating supervision quality separately from acting ability.

02Nov 11

WorldDraft tests whether video models preserve programmed worlds

WorldDraft evaluates video models as renderers of worlds already specified by code. Its benchmark contains 432 paired cases across 48 executable world families, testing preservation of motion and camera paths alongside visual appearance. Across 12 models, the authors report tension between structural fidelity and appearance control, including source leakage reaching 64% under conflicting cues. A visual prompting method called WorldDraft-Primer improves both objectives, targeting more controllable simulation-to-video workflows.

04Nov 11

Reinforcement learning optimizes what agents hand to future attempts

Experience-Handoff Reinforcement Learning trains an agent to improve not only actions but also the experience artifacts passed to later rollouts. Under a fixed Agentic Context Engineering harness, one model acts, reflects, and curates task-local playbooks. Candidate playbooks are evaluated by their effect on future performance. The authors report better held-out benchmark results than baselines, suggesting that useful memory construction can itself be a learning target rather than a fixed prompting convention.

05Nov 11

DeepStress probes search agents with unreliable evidence

DeepStress replaces retrieval with a controlled synthetic environment to test search agents under degraded evidence. It varies document trustworthiness, relevance, and factuality independently, and evaluates 12 systems using HotpotQA and BrowseComp-Plus questions. The authors find that some open-weight agents answer confidently despite obvious credibility warnings, while many questions can be answered from memorized knowledge even when retrieved documents are wrong. New metrics reward caution and account for computational cost.

06Nov 10

JustRUA isolates harness choices behind robot-use performance

JustRUA studies GPT-6 Astra robot-use agents under a common framework, separating harness design, action interfaces, experience reuse, and scene geometry. The authors report that retaining full episode history and stopping motions early helps, while scene geometry provides the largest benefit. A simplified harness raises the RoboDojo simulation score from 29.7 to 50.3 without a learned policy or task-specific tuning. These are simulation results, not evidence of equivalent performance in physical deployment.

Agent-R² trains a 4B productivity agent on 4,000 grounded tasks

Agent-R² builds productivity training tasks from occupational seeds, workflow evidence, and authentic web-sourced files, pairing deliverables with detailed evaluation rubrics. From 10,000 candidates, the authors distill 4,000 tasks and train a 4B model with supervised learning and rubric-based reinforcement learning. They report 51.0% on GDPval and 14.8% on OfficeQA, exceeding the cited Qwen3-235B baseline. The study emphasizes sustained grounding and verification over producing superficially plausible work products.

08Nov 4

Robot self-improvement can overfit exposed success criteria

A controlled study examines whether exposing benchmark success criteria improves embodied agents' task skills or mainly teaches them to satisfy a verifier. Agents given the criteria rapidly obtain higher measured success, but the advantage disappears when criteria change slightly while preserving the original instructions. Their learned skills also transfer poorly across tasks. The authors propose instruction-faithful criteria, highlighting a distinction between improving benchmark scores and learning behavior that generalizes beyond one evaluator.

09Nov 11

FOAM reduces the cost of SOAP-style optimization

FOAM is a lightweight variant of SOAP that uses input-only preconditioning and a factored estimate of the rotated second moment. The authors report validation losses competitive with SOAP and Muon, half of SOAP's optimizer-state memory, and Muon-class wall-clock time. Experiments span language-model pretraining from 124 million to 760 million parameters. The results suggest a practical efficiency trade-off, while leaving performance at much larger frontier training scales an open question.
