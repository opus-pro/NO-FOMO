MMFlash targets visually dependent tokens to accelerate VLM decoding

MMFlash studies why speculative decoding becomes harder when token predictions depend strongly on visual input. The authors report that draft-token acceptance falls from 46 percent in the lowest visual-dependence quartile to 22 percent in the highest. Their method upweights difficult visually dependent tokens and adds sparse attention for long videos. Across the evaluated multimodal benchmarks, they report lossless decoding speedups of up to 3.58 times, making this a concrete inference-efficiency result to investigate.

Continual-ARC separates persistent memory from transferable learning

Continual-ARC evaluates lifelong learning using recurring tasks from three ARC-AGI editions, with fresh instances, feedback budgets, and later tests on both familiar and unseen tasks. The study compares agent harnesses with memory and weight-based learning on a shared scale. In the reported experiments, persistence generally fails to improve memorization without expert guidance, and harness methods do not gain on unseen tasks. The results challenge the assumption that retaining interaction history automatically produces generalizable skills.
