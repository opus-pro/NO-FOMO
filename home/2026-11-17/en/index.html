04Nov 17

Large harness study finds 64.5% of inference cost goes to unsuccessful runs

Runta and Baseten evaluated 22 harness configurations with five models across 62 tasks and four attempts, totaling 27,280 agent runs. Their report attributes 64.5% of estimated list-price inference cost to failures or timeouts. The best-value harness varied by model, and higher spending did not reliably buy higher pass rates. These findings support testing model and harness combinations together, while the study's open-model set, provider pricing, and task selection limit generalization.

02Nov 14

SWE-CoWork exposes a gap between partial code and complete delivery

SWE-CoWork evaluates coding agents on 101 assignments in simulated organizations where requirements are distributed across colleagues and records. The two leading models passed roughly 63% of fail-to-pass tests but completed fewer than 8% of whole assignments. Supplying complete requirements improved some test results without improving complete-task success. The study points to persistent tracking failures across clarification, implementation, verification, and handoff, making organizational task completion a tougher target than isolated patch generation.

03Nov 10

Termination Lag measures work after coding agents already solve a task

A study across five models, two frameworks, and three SWE-bench datasets measures how long coding agents continue after their first correct patch. Between 38% and 82% of successful runs already had a correct solution in the initial source edit. Substantial later effort went into verification and exploration, and 1% to 8% of cases lost an earlier correct patch. The findings argue for better stopping decisions while preserving checks that catch real defects.

04Nov 3

SPILLWAY uses typed tool provenance to constrain injected action targets

SPILLWAY proposes a deterministic check on action targets for tool-using agents, using typed values returned by read-only tools and fixed delegation rules. It separates target admission from sanitizing suspicious free text, aiming to keep the final permission decision outside model interpretation. The authors report no redirected actions under standard templated attacks in their evaluation and low success for a learned attacker. These are results within tested suites, not proof against every prompt-injection strategy.

05Nov 5

BitDecoding-FP4 combines quantization and kernels for faster serving

BitDecoding-FP4 tackles accuracy and throughput together for native four-bit key-value cache decoding. Its design addresses query quantization error, probability underflow, and key outliers, then aligns those changes with attention kernels. The authors report accuracy close to BF16 caches and 2.4 to 3.0 times higher peak serving throughput on NVIDIA B300 with two Qwen3 models. The result is hardware- and workload-specific, but illustrates why numerical methods and implementation need to be evaluated together.

06Nov 15

MovieRoll targets sustained quality in longer generated videos

MovieRoll combines streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and inference strategies to sustain video quality beyond the training horizon. Hierarchical key-value cache compression reportedly reduces cache memory by up to 78%, while a long-rollout training method supports a few-step generator. On HeliosBench, the authors report strong distilled-model scores on sequences up to 60 seconds. The contribution addresses both accumulating generation errors and growing memory demands, with the reported comparisons tied to that benchmark.

LynnReal-Hand explores hand-controlled streaming video worlds

LynnReal-Hand introduces a video world model controlled through hand movements, trained with 100,000 real and synthetic interaction clips. 08Nov 15

ScaleVLA tests a 552B backbone for robot control

ScaleVLA connects a 552-billion-parameter DeepSeek backbone to a trainable action diffusion transformer and compares model sizes under a shared setup. The authors report better sample efficiency and generalization, including 45.1% success on LIBERO-PRO and improved charger insertion in a small real-world test. Median latency was 216 milliseconds per action chunk. The results suggest larger representations can help robot policies, while the limited physical trial count warrants caution about broader deployment performance.
