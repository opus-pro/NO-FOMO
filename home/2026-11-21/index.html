01Nov 21

Lean community certifies Riemann Hypothesis proof

The Lean and Mathlib communities say they have verified Levent Alpöge's Riemann Hypothesis proof, released as a 17-million-line Lean library. Their joint statement reports reproducible compilation, agreement between the proved theorem and Mathlib's vetted statement, and successful checks by several independent proof checkers. This is a major formal-verification development. The statement also looks ahead to explaining the proof's mathematical ideas; machine verification and a human-readable mathematical explanation remain distinct achievements.

SimpleBench keeps everyday reasoning gaps in focus

A Hacker News discussion draws attention to SimpleBench amid new frontier-model releases. The benchmark covers more than 200 multiple-choice questions in spatial and temporal reasoning, social intelligence, and resistance to linguistic traps. Its website reports an 83.7 percent human baseline from nine participants and 81.9 percent for its leading listed model, Claude Fable. The small human sample and benchmark-specific setup matter: these figures expose uneven capabilities without settling overall model usefulness.

A practical guide explores privately hosted open-weight AI

Micah Lee's guide describes using open-weight models through Maple's chat, desktop, and API interfaces, presenting trusted execution environments as the provider's privacy mechanism.
