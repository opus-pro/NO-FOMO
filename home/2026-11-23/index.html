01Nov 20

StereoVLM grounds spatial reasoning in calibrated binocular vision

StereoVLM introduces a binocular vision-language model that learns metric spatial reasoning from calibrated stereo observations. Its training progresses from correspondence and disparity to depth and semantic reasoning, with a new Stereo3D-Bench evaluation. The authors report 95.17 percent depth accuracy and improvements on spatial tasks and RoboCasa manipulation. Their intervention experiments suggest valid cross-view geometry contributes to performance. This makes the work relevant to embodied systems that need grounded distances rather than plausible visual descriptions alone.

02Nov 19

MemOnce compresses reusable memory for long-context models

MemOnce proposes a memory encoder-decoder that stores a small subset of final-layer states and reconstructs attention caches when needed. A sparse retrieval mechanism computes memory indices once across layers, targeting the cost of continual interactions. The authors report that adapting Qwen3.5-4B reduces memory-state cache by 1,313 times and prefill latency by 6.9 times at a million-token context. These are experimental results for the reported setup, with deployment value depending on retained task quality and workload characteristics.

03Nov 19

NoPeWorld targets collisions in video world models

NoPeWorld tackles object penetration in interactive video world models using a small prediction head on frozen diffusion-transformer hidden states. It detects unsafe candidate actions and replaces them with alternatives intended to preserve the original action's purpose. Across six video world models, the authors report reducing average physical violations from 74 percent to 16 percent. The accompanying NoPeBench, code, and weights make this a concrete research direction for improving physical consistency, while the remaining violations show the problem is not solved.

04Nov 18

AgentRoboCritic examines robot-control failures action by action

AgentRoboCritic analyzes frontier agents' direct robot control across twelve manipulation benchmarks, asking the policy to state an intent alongside each numerical action. A critic then evaluates outcomes and failure modes against that intent. The study identifies recovery and semantic-grounding strengths alongside grasping and alignment bottlenecks. It releases 12,844 annotated actions and an open critic, with reported failure-mode accuracy improving from 32.58 percent to 74.24 percent. The contribution is diagnostic visibility beyond a single episode-success score.

05Nov 18

Duopra trains voice interaction and tool execution together

Duopra extends full-duplex conversation toward task execution by training timely tool calls and revisions alongside conversational control. Its three-stage process combines supervised protocol training, reinforcement learning, and repair of talker conditioning. With the same backend, the authors report Pass@1 rising from 4.7 percent to 44.7 percent in their streaming evaluation. The study argues that many failures occur before a tool runs, making faithful request initiation as important as the backend's ability to complete a task.

06Nov 18

QueryLM learns search during pretraining

Search-Native Pretraining augments ordinary text corpora with search queries and retrieved responses, teaching models to call search and use evidence before post-training. Across experiments from 360 million to 3.3 billion parameters, the authors report improved knowledge-task performance and stronger responsiveness to retrieved material. A 1.4-billion-parameter QueryLM matches or beats a compute-matched 3.3-billion baseline on tested knowledge-intensive tasks. Released checkpoints and data support examination of whether tool use should be learned earlier in the model pipeline.

07Nov 16

Motion-aware rewards address frozen-looking video edits

A video-editing study finds that reward models can prefer suppressed motion while appearing to reward temporal consistency. On its controlled VE-Bench comparison, an evaluated composite reward favored motion-suppressed candidates in 97 percent of cases. The proposed reward adds frame-difference inputs and training against suppressed or fabricated motion, reducing that preference to zero in the reported test. The result highlights a specific reward-design failure: optimizing a convenient quality signal can remove the movement users wanted to preserve.
